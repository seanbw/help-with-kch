CARE EXPERIENCEPERSONAL ACCOUNT

Falling into; coming to

When I was taking Oxycodone, I could usually manage the drowsiness by getting up, stretching my legs or making a cup of tea. After I was prescribed Tapentadol, I began to experience something different: I was still in pain, then I would open my eyes and realise I had lost track of what happened in between.

At first, I could not understand why the familiar stages of getting tired, closing my eyes and falling asleep seemed to disappear. These episodes began happening during the day as well as at night. I eventually connected the change to Tapentadol and raised my concerns with the pain team, asking to discuss another treatment. I did not feel that my concerns were taken seriously.

The episodes affected my everyday life. I lost a job I cared about after repeatedly becoming unresponsive at work, and after one frightening episode in my car I decided I could no longer drive. I am sharing what I experienced and the questions I tried to raise, not offering a diagnosis or advice about anyone else's medication.

My concerns grew after carers found me confused and disoriented during a visit. I wanted the change in my behaviour to be followed up, and I wanted a careful conversation about the medication and its effects on me.

This is one patient's recollection and perspective, not medical advice or a finding about every clinician. Medication concerns should be discussed with a qualified health professional.

POLICY & COMMUNICATION

When asking for a policy becomes a struggle

In November 2025, I asked the hospital for a copy of its medicines policy. I wanted to understand the rules around medicines that had become an important part of my care and to have something clear to refer to when asking questions.

The process felt harder than it should have been. Patients should be able to ask how a policy affects them and receive a clear, respectful response. I write about my experience because access to information and open communication matter, especially when a patient is worried about their treatment.

03 Who took
my Fifi?

OTHER SYSTEMS

Who took my Fifi?

Another ongoing story, this time about housing, a disputed notice and trying to make sense of a court process while unwell. I was given one notice in May 2023. Later, a second notice said to be from the same day was raised in court, although I had not received it. The dispute left me facing a debt I could not manage.

It is part of the wider picture of how hard it can be to feel heard by systems with power over your life.

This account is ongoing and reflects my understanding of events.
